## What the spec actually grades

The report needs evidence — screenshots, chat logs — of how the team used generative AI, **and**
a write-up of how the team reviewed and evaluated that output. The penalty attaches to
*unreviewed output that reached the deliverable*, not to the volume of use. Nothing caps how
much AI we may use; the course encourages it.

## Your job, from Sep 21

Keep some evidence of your interactions with AI. A screenshot or a couple of descriptive sentences would be fine.

## The single most valuable thing to save

**The outputs you rejected.** A hallucinated figure you caught, a requirement that contradicted
the World Assumptions Masterlist, a traceability link the tool asserted that did not hold —
these are direct proof of the review process the spec asks us to write up. Most teams throw
these away and keep only the good outputs, which is exactly backwards. Saving them costs
nothing.

## Also log the decisions only a person made

These are real, and there are many on this project:

- **Scope calls** — which assumptions from the World Assumptions Masterlist we adopted, added,
  or dropped, and why.
- **Issue selection** — which candidate issues were kept, which were cut, on what criterion.
- **Design rationale** — the spec asks *why* each resolution was chosen.
- **The Top Priority scenario choice**, and the reasoning behind it.
- **Corrections made** — every place the output was wrong and someone fixed it.

## Assembling D-6

The owner and co-author merge the five logs into one report:

1. **Tools used**, and for which work products.
2. **How the team used them** — a short paragraph per work product, drawn from the logs.
3. **Review and evaluation process** — the SC-5 verification rule, who checked what, against
   which source documents. The spec explicitly asks for this, and teams most often skip it.
4. **What the team caught** — concrete rejected or corrected examples, with screenshots. Three
   or four good ones beat an exhaustive list.
5. **Evidence appendix** — selected screenshots and log excerpts.
